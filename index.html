Most small businesses don't have a bookkeeping problem. They have a clarity problem.

The shoebox

Receipts in an inbox, a drawer and three different apps. Nothing is wrong exactly — nothing is filed either, and year-end is coming.

Books that don't match the bank

The ledger says one number, the bank says another, and nobody has reconciled the difference in months. Every decision rests on a figure you don't quite trust.

No idea if this month was good

Money came in and money went out. Whether you actually made a profit — and where it went — is genuinely unclear until the accountant tells you, far too late to act.

Why work with me

I learned bookkeeping from the other side of the lending desk.

Before I kept books, I judged them. Eight years inside commercial banks and a credit rating agency, deciding whether a business's numbers could be trusted.

01

Credit and risk consciousness

At BRAC Bank I underwrote and structured commercial credit; at CRISL I wrote rating reports on banks, insurers, garment and textile manufacturers. I know precisely which entries a lender scrutinises, which ratios they compute, and which sloppy classifications sink an otherwise healthy application. Your books get kept to that standard from the start — so the year you need financing, you are already ready.
