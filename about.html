The short version

Why a former banker keeps books.

I help small businesses and solo entrepreneurs stay on top of their finances with accurate, timely bookkeeping — so they can focus on running the business instead of chasing numbers.

Before moving into bookkeeping I spent eight years in corporate and relationship banking. At BRAC Bank and Habib Bank I analysed financial statements, monitored credit portfolios and worked directly with business owners on their financing needs. Earlier, at CRISL, I wrote credit rating reports on banks, insurers, garment and textile manufacturers. All of it came down to one repeated question: can these numbers be believed?

That is an unusual foundation for a bookkeeper, and it is the whole point. I know how to read a financial statement, where errors like to hide, and precisely what lenders and business owners need to see. When I categorise a transaction I am thinking about how it will read on your Profit & Loss twelve months from now, in front of someone deciding whether to lend you money.

Today I am trained and certified in QuickBooks Online, Xero and Wave, and I work with clients entirely remotely — either on an ongoing monthly engagement or as a one-off clean-up project.

How I work

What you can expect from me.

Confidentiality first

Financial data is handled with the discretion banking taught me. I work from accountant-level access to your systems and never ask for online banking passwords.

Predictable rhythm

We agree a cadence — daily, weekly or monthly — and I hold to it. Your month-end pack arrives on the same schedule, not whenever it happens to be ready.

Plain language

Reports come with a short written commentary in ordinary English. If something in your numbers concerns me, you hear about it in the month it happens.

Honest scoping

If your books need three months of clean-up, I say so before we start rather than discovering it later. No surprise scope, no surprise invoices.
